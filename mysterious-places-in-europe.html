Mysterious Places in Europe

These 32 sites in europe are ancient, unexplained or still debated by historians and scientists. Each page explains what is known, what is disputed and where to read more.

All 32 places

  • Stonehenge – A ring of standing stones raised around 3000–2000 BCE. Some “bluestones” were hauled about 150 miles from Wales. Why it was built is still…
  • Loch Ness – Reports of a creature in the loch date back to a 6th‑century account of St Columba. The famous 1934 “Surgeon’s Photo” was later admitted to be a…
  • Avebury – Largest stone circle in Europe, built around 2500 BCE.
  • Newgrange – Irish passage tomb older than the pyramids, lit by the winter solstice sunrise.
  • Skara Brae – Neolithic village in Orkney preserved under sand for millennia.
  • Ring of Brodgar – Orkney stone circle whose ritual purpose is unknown.
  • Carnac stones – Over 3,000 standing stones in Brittany; purpose still debated.
  • Externsteine – German rock formation with ancient carvings and unclear ritual use.
  • Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum – Underground Maltese temple of about 3300 BCE with unusual acoustics.
  • Ġgantija – Maltese megalithic temples older than Stonehenge.
  • Su Nuraxi di Barumini – Sardinian Bronze Age tower complex whose builders left no writing.
  • Knossos – Minoan palace linked to the Labyrinth and Minotaur myth.
  • Akrotiri (Santorini) – Bronze Age town buried by a volcanic eruption; tied to Atlantis tales.
  • Delphi – Site of the oracle where the Pythia gave prophecies.
  • Antikythera shipwreck – Wreck where an ancient astronomical computer was found in 1900.
  • Pompeii – Roman city frozen in time by Vesuvius in 79 CE.
  • Hessdalen lights – Norwegian valley with recurring unexplained lights.
  • Bonnybridge – Scottish town known as a UFO hotspot.
  • Rendlesham Forest – Site of the 1980 UFO incident near a US air base.
  • Kola Superdeep Borehole – Deepest hole ever drilled, surrounded by myths.
  • Pripyat – Abandoned city beside Chernobyl.
  • Rennes-le-Château – French village at the centre of hidden treasure theories.
  • Glastonbury Tor – Hill linked to Avalon and Arthurian legend.
  • Tintagel – Legendary birthplace of King Arthur.
  • Rollright Stones – Stones said to be a king and his men turned to rock.
  • Cerne Abbas Giant – Hill figure of uncertain date.
  • Uffington White Horse – 3,000-year-old chalk figure.
  • Silbury Hill – Largest man-made prehistoric mound in Europe.
  • Callanish Stones – Hebridean stone setting with lunar alignments.
  • Lascaux – Paleolithic cave paintings in France.
  • Altamira – Spanish cave with paintings about 36,000 years old.
  • Chauvet Cave – Some of the oldest known cave paintings.
